DESCRIPTION
A brown government envelope sits on a kitchen table in Kiryas Joel, New York. Eleven people live in the house. Everyone who hears the question pictures the same thing: a switch in an office, someone flips it, and the money for this community stops.

There is no switch. Means-tested benefits in the United States go to households, not communities, and they are set by income and household size. This documentary covers what a real cut would have to look like, and why the $100,000 figure is an attributed claim rather than a verified payment. It then turns to the lever that does exist: the "substantially equivalent" education standard and the school funding attached to it. It also looks at the institutions the community runs for itself (Hatzalah, Shomrim, Chaveirim, the Beth Din, free-loan societies), at who would actually absorb a cut, at the strongest case the critics make, including former members, and at what a similar envelope would reveal about the rest of us.

Sources named on screen include the YAFFED report (2018–2022), U.S. Census figures (Kiryas Joel, 2010: 91.5% speak Yiddish at home) and reporting in The New York Times. Figures marked "illustrative" are worked examples, not official amounts.
