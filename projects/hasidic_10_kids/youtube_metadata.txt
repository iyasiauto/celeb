DESCRIPTION
In Kiryas Joel, New York, the median age is 15.7. The poverty rate is close to 38 percent, and families with eight, nine or ten children are normal. So how do Hasidic families afford ten kids without jobs?

The question turns out to be half wrong. This documentary goes through the census numbers, university studies and federal court records. It shows that most Hasidic men in America do work, usually in small businesses for modest wages. It then covers the economic system built around them: benefits scaled to household size, low-cost yeshiva schooling, interest-free gemach loans, community charity, and dense shared housing. It also covers the documented fraud cases, and a different definition of wealth.
