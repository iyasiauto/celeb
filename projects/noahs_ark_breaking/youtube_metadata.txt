DESCRIPTION
In September 2026 a drilling crew in eastern Turkey drove a percussion bit into the Durupınar formation, the boat-shaped outline some researchers believe is Noah's Ark. Four to five metres down, the bit shattered. The team says the layer is "harder than limestone" and could be petrified wood. The story went everywhere within days.

This video goes through what can actually be verified: the permitted, university-supervised expedition, the press release, the cores and the broken bit. It covers the site's 66-year history from the 1959 NATO photographs to Ron Wyatt and David Fasold's testimony under oath. It asks why this hillside, and weighs the scans, the soil and the drilling against the published geology. Then it sets out the laboratory tests that could settle it this winter.
