TITLE
Why 20,000 Hasidic Jews Were Told To Stay Silent About Crime?

ALTERNATIVE TITLES
The Rule That Made Calling the Police a Sin — Mesirah, Explained
Inside Brooklyn's Code of Silence: The Rule Called Mesirah

DESCRIPTION
In December 2012, a seventeen-year-old girl testified in a Brooklyn courtroom about years of abuse — and men in the public benches photographed her for the neighbourhood back home. How does calling the police become the crime?

This investigation follows a rule with a name — mesirah, the ancient prohibition on handing a fellow Jew to the outside power — from the centuries of persecution that forged it, to the rabbinical rulings that said it does not cover abusers, to the machinery that kept the silence in place anyway: gatekeeping, wall posters, shunning, delay, and a city that met the silence halfway.

It is not a story about one community. It is a story about what truth costs when the price of telling it is every person you have.

Sources: The New York Times (2012 investigation of the Brooklyn DA's Kol Tzedek program); JTA and J. Weekly (2011 rulings and statements: the Crown Heights beis din, the Rabbinical Council of America, Agudath Israel of America); DNAinfo, Brooklyn Eagle and court reporting on People v. Weberman; JTA (2020) on pashkevilin; The Forward.
